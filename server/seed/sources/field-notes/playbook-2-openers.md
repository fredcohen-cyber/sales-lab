### From the field: how Omnizant’s booked calls opened

Fourteen recorded Omnizant outbound calls each ended with a booked meeting, most often a 15- to 20-minute website review. They show what worked, not how often it works. These are the moves they shared.

- **Respect their time up front.** *“I’ll make it quick, I know you’re busy,”* or *“I want to be very respectful of your time.”* That is rule 2 on page one, applied to a busy attorney.
- **Ask for the relationship, not the decision-maker.** *“Who there handles the relationship between your firm and [FindLaw / Justia / Scorpion / your website provider]?”* Add a name if you have one: *“Is that [name], or someone else?”* Saying you don’t expect it to be the partner can help: after *“I don’t think it’ll be [partner’s first name]; it’s probably someone else,”* one receptionist put the rep straight through to the person who updates the website.
- **Confirm the role before you pitch.** *“If the firm added a partner, would you be the one to change the website? Do you work with the company that hosts it?”*
- **Get past screens with a plain reason.** In a phone menu, choose the marketing or vendor option when there is one, and say that you did. To an AI call screener, give a short operational reason and your name: *“Regarding the firm’s website.”*
- **Open with a reason that’s about them.** The openers that booked named something specific to the firm: a change at their provider (*“Have you noticed any changes since FindLaw was sold?”*), an affiliation the firm trusts, or something you noticed on their website, with a genuine compliment first: *“I came across your website and I actually really like it. I did have a couple of thoughts.”* Then ask about their experience and let them describe the problem.
- **Offer a small, specific next step instead of a pitch.** A free 15-minute review of their website, *“not right now, next week sometime,”* with the pressure taken off: *“It’s not going to be a high-pressure sales call. For all I know, everything’s great.”* If the review will stay a review, say so: *“If you want our services after that, that’ll be a separate call.”*
- **Be candid about where you stand.** *“I know I just called out of the blue”* and *“I’ll be upfront, I haven’t looked closely at your website yet”* made reps sound like people, not scripts.
- **Make your name easy to find.** Spell it (*“O-M-N-I-Z-A-N-T; it doesn’t roll off the tongue”*), then wait quietly while they look you up. Prospects who searched for the company mid-call still booked.

Two cautions from the same calls:

- **Keep a provider hook factual.** Thomson Reuters sold FindLaw to Internet Brands; the sale closed in December 2024. Say what other firms report and ask about this firm’s experience; don’t tell them their service got worse.
- **Use an affiliation only where it applies, and name the company first.** Reps describe Omnizant as a State Bar of Texas member benefit partner, which is an opener for Texas firms only. Say “Omnizant” before the affiliation, including to a receptionist, so the call can’t be mistaken for one from the Bar.
