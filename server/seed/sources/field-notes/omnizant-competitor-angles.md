How reps opened and handled calls with firms on four website providers, from fourteen recorded Omnizant outbound calls. Say what firms report and ask about this firm’s experience. Never state a competitor’s weakness as fact, and never criticize a competitor.

### FindLaw

**The fact:** Thomson Reuters sold FindLaw to Internet Brands; the sale closed in December 2024.

**The opener:** *“Since FindLaw was sold, a lot of firms have told us the service hasn’t been the same. Have you noticed any changes?”* Prospects filled in the rest: no contact from the new owners; referrals no better than before (*“I still get the same lousy referrals that I used to get”*); and, when asked, one firm said it paid $1,700 to $1,800 a month for site administration, SEO, and updates and found it hard to tell how much work that bought.

**Watch for:** staff too new to compare before and after. Offer the review as peace of mind about the site they now manage.

### Justia

**The opener:** name the provider and offer the review: *“I noticed you have a website with Justia. Would you be open to a 15- or 20-minute review of it?”* No criticism of Justia was needed to book.

### Scorpion

**The opener:** *“A lot of firms tell us that initially things are great, then they slowly drop off as time goes on. What has your experience been?”*

**What to expect:** Scorpion clients may be tired of these calls, including from former Scorpion employees, and wary of switching: the comfort of a long relationship, worry about downtime, rebuild fees (one attorney mentioned quotes of about $4,000 for a new site plus a monthly fee), and the sense that AI can now build a site for free. Agree that anyone can build a good-looking site, then talk about capturing and converting the leads it brings.

**Competing firms:** on a call with a Justia firm, one rep said Scorpion was *“most likely also helping your competition.”* Don’t repeat it; it is an unverified claim about a competitor. If working with local competitors matters to the buyer, describe only Omnizant’s own policy, once it is confirmed.

### LawLytics

**The situation:** on one call, a LawLytics firm’s administrator said it was *“basically using them to host our website, and that’s about it,”* and wanted to stop spending so much.

**The comparison the rep led with:** who owns the site. Ask rather than assert: *“What platform is your site built on, and what does your agreement say happens to your site and content if you leave?”* Then cover what else the firm would get each month.

**What booked the meeting:** when the administrator said he would talk it over with the attorney, the rep offered a short screen-share of sites moved from LawLytics, and he agreed.

**Respect the competitor:** *“LawLytics, to their credit, has been very professional in the handoff process.”* It tells the buyer that switching won’t be a fight.
