How reps opened and handled calls with firms on four website providers, from fourteen recorded Omnizant calls in October 2026. Say what firms report and ask about this firm’s experience; never state a competitor’s weakness as fact about the firm you’re calling.

### FindLaw

**The fact:** Thomson Reuters sold FindLaw to Internet Brands; the sale closed in December 2024.

**The opener:** *“A lot of firms have been switching to us since FindLaw was sold, and they tell us the service hasn’t been the same. Have you noticed any changes?”* Prospects volunteered the rest: no contact from the new owners, *“the same lousy referrals,”* and one firm paying $1,700 to $1,800 a month for site administration and SEO without being sure how much work it bought.

**Watch for:** staff too new to compare before and after. Offer the review as peace of mind about the site they now manage.

### Justia

**The opener:** name the provider and offer the review: *“I noticed you have a website with Justia. Would you be open to a 15- or 20-minute review of it?”* No criticism of Justia was needed to book.

### Scorpion

**The opener:** *“A lot of firms tell us that initially things are great, then they slowly drop off as time goes on. What has your experience been?”*

**What to expect:** Scorpion clients may be tired of these calls, including from former Scorpion employees, and wary of switching: the comfort of a long relationship, worry about downtime, rebuild fees (one attorney mentioned quotes of about $4,000 for a new site plus a monthly fee), and the sense that AI can now build a site for free. Agree that anyone can build a good-looking site, then talk about capturing and converting the leads it brings.

**Competing firms:** one rep contrasted Omnizant with agencies that may also work for a firm’s local competitors. Ask whether that matters to the buyer rather than asserting what another agency does in their market.

### LawLytics

**The situation:** reps have reached LawLytics firms that mainly use it for hosting and maintenance and feel they pay too much for that alone.

**The comparison that worked:** who owns the site. LawLytics runs firm websites on its own platform, so ask what the firm’s agreement says happens to its site and content if it leaves. Then cover what else the firm would get each month, and offer proof: a short screen-share of sites moved from LawLytics.

**Respect the competitor:** *“LawLytics, to their credit, has been very professional in the handoff process.”* It tells the buyer that switching won’t be a fight.
