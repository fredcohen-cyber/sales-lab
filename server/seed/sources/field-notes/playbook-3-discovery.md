### From the field: discovery on a cold call

On a cold call, discovery has one job: find a reason to meet. Omnizant’s booked calls asked at most two or three questions, and many booked on the offer alone; the full sequence above belongs in the meeting.

- **Ask one open question, then follow the answer.** *“Have you noticed any changes?”* When a partner said the new owners of his provider had never contacted him, the rep asked a second question, *“Have you seen any changes in the traffic you’re getting?”*, and heard the real problem: *“I still get the same lousy referrals.”*
- **Ask what they pay, and what it buys.** *“Do you know by chance how much you’re paying monthly?”* One firm was paying $1,700 to $1,800 a month and found it hard to tell how much work that bought. The rep asked what it covered (site administration, SEO, and updates), offered to break down what the firm was paying for and show some of Omnizant’s work, and the prospect asked for a meeting to go over Omnizant’s services herself.
- **Name what you hear.** When an attorney said his website was just where he sent people after meeting them, the rep said, *“Sounds like it’s almost like a digital business card for you,”* and he agreed. The rep then offered a credibility and health check instead of marketing.
- **Steer from volume to fit.** *“People think you just need leads. It’s about having the right ones; I’m sure there’s a type of case you prefer.”*
