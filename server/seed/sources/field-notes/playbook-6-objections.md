### From the field: objections on Omnizant’s booked calls

Each of these came up on a call that still ended with a meeting.

| They said | What worked |
| --- | --- |
| “I haven’t been here long enough to notice.” | Offer the review for their sake: peace of mind about the site they now manage. |
| “Is there a charge?” | *“No, it’s a free review. If you want our services after that, that’ll be a separate call.”* |
| “What do you get out of it?” | Be upfront: it’s how you get your name out, you do sell these services, and the findings are useful to whoever works on the site, whether or not that’s you. |
| “Do you need any access or information from me?” | No logins or data. The review uses what’s public, such as load time, links, the Google Business Profile, and reviews. All you need is 15 minutes. |
| “Send me something so I can check you out” (after the meeting is booked) | Agree right away: send the invite within a couple of minutes, then a follow-up with your website and credentials. Before a meeting is booked, use the “Just send me some information” play above. |
| “You’re not much cheaper than who we have.” | Don’t argue price. Briefly lay out what would change for them, then offer proof: a short screen-share of sites you’ve moved from that provider. On the call, the long answer ended in “I’ll talk it over”; the screen-share offer booked the meeting. |
| “I’ll talk it over with [the attorney] and get back to you.” | Offer the short screen-share and book it on the call. The rep booked the administrator alone; the “I need to talk to my partner” play above would also put the attorney on the invite. |
| “With AI I can build a site for free.” | Agree, then move from the website to what happens after a visitor lands: *“Just like you said, anybody can make a great-looking website.”* Add that they may walk away with things they can do themselves, such as on their Google Business Profile. |
| “Never heard of you.” | Give one grounding fact, such as where you’re based or how long you’ve been around, spell the name, and let them look you up. |
| “Do you have other firms in my state?” | Say only what you know (*“I couldn’t tell you how many off the top of my head”*) and offer to check. Don’t guess a number, don’t say “I’m sure we do” unless you know, and don’t answer with a claim about a competitor. |

Each answer lowered the risk of a 15-minute meeting and went straight back to booking it. None of these calls isolated the objection first (step 2 of the pattern above); on a first call, the meeting is the ask.
