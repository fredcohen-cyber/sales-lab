### From the field: objections on Omnizant’s booked calls

Each of these came up on a call that still ended with a meeting.

| They said | What worked |
| --- | --- |
| “I haven’t been here long enough to notice.” | Offer the review for their sake: peace of mind about the site they now manage. |
| “Is there a charge?” | *“No, it’s a free review. If you want our services after that, that’ll be a separate call.”* |
| “What do you get out of it?” | Be upfront: it’s how you get your name out, and the findings are useful to whoever works on the site, whether or not that’s you. |
| “Do you need any access or information from me?” | No logins or data. The review uses what’s public, such as load time, links, the Google Business Profile, and reviews. All you need is 15 minutes. |
| “Send me something so I can check you out.” | Agree right away, and send your website and credentials within minutes, along with the invite. |
| “You’re not much cheaper than who we have.” | Don’t argue price. Lay out what would change for them, then offer proof: a short screen-share of sites you’ve moved from that provider. |
| “I’ll talk it over with my partner and get back to you.” | Offer the short screen-share and book it on the call. Better still, invite the partner too (see “I need to talk to my partner” above). |
| “With AI I can build a site for free.” | Agree, then move from the website to what happens after a visitor lands: *“Just like you said, anybody can make a great-looking website.”* Offer something they can do themselves, such as a Google Business Profile fix. |
| “Never heard of you.” | Give one grounding fact, such as where you’re based or how long you’ve been around, spell the name, and let them look you up. |
| “Do you have other firms in my state?” | Answer honestly (*“I couldn’t tell you how many off the top of my head”*); never guess a number. |

Each answer took the risk out of a 15-minute meeting instead of defending the company or the price: the pattern above, applied to a first call, with the meeting as the ask.
