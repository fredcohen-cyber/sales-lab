### From the field: closing for the review meeting

Each of Omnizant’s booked calls closed for a dated meeting, not a sale. These habits made the meeting stick.

- **Book it while you’re both on the phone.** *“Do you have your calendar in front of you?”* Then offer a specific day and two times, leaving room for court: *“10:00 or 11:00, or after court at 1:00?”*
- **Turn “call me back” into a time.** When a prospect said she’d never heard of the company and asked for a call tomorrow, the rep asked, *“What time are you available tomorrow? Would 11:00 to 11:15 work?”*, and sent the invite.
- **Settle the time zone out loud.** *“What time is it there right now?”* Leave a buffer after the time they give: one rep booked 11:30 when the partner said “after 10:30.”
- **Verify the email on the call.** Read it back, use a phonetic alphabet when needed, and send to the address they choose.
- **Make the invite land.** When you can, send it while they’re still on the line and wait until they see it. Otherwise: *“Accept it so it shows on your calendar. If I don’t see it accepted, I’ll call you tomorrow, because sometimes it goes to spam.”* Tell them whose name to look for, and use a plain subject such as *“Website Review: [your name] at Omnizant.”*
- **Read the time back from the invite, not from memory.** Two calls ended with the rep repeating the wrong time.
- **Put everyone who decides on the invite,** and say who is joining from your side with one line of credibility: a website specialist, or the Director of Sales and how long she has been with the company.
- **Remind them when it’s far out.** For a meeting a week or more away, send a reminder the day before.
